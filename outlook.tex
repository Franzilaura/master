\section{Outlook}


The original research conducted in this thesis has led us to
define operadic fibrations (c.f. Definition \ref{def:operadicfib}) that
are equivalent to colored braided operads in $\Set$ (c.f. \ref{def:col-br-op}). However, our definition of colored braided operads
can be stated with values in any symmetric monoidal category. This allows us to finally think about futher generalizations of our definition of operadic fibration as well as colored braided operad.
In fact, we think that
dropping in Definition \ref{def:operadicfib} the condition:
\begin{itemize}
    \item [(iii)] For a morphism $\widetilde{\alpha}$ in $\Brx$, the groupoid of
    morphisms lying over $\widetilde{\alpha}$:
    $$\Hom_{\D}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}(X, X')$$ is discrete.
\end{itemize}
could lead us to an equivalence to colored braided operads
in $\Grp$.
Perhaps a further generalization could lead us to the following definition of a braided $\infty$-operad:
\begin{defin} 
    A braided $\infty${\em-operad} is a functor $p \colon \Bx \to \nerv(\Brx)$
    between $\infty$-categories satisfying the following conditions:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
         \item The functor $p$ is a categorical fibration (c.f. Definition \cite[Corollary 2.4.6.5]{hht}.

        \item For every inert morphism $\widetilde{\alpha} \colon \langle n \rangle
        \to \langle m \rangle$ in $\Brx$ and object $X \in \Bx$ there exists a
        $\pi$-coCartesian morphism $f \colon X \to X'$ lifting $\widetilde{\alpha}$. In
        particular, $\widetilde{\alpha}$ induces a functor: $$\pi_{\widetilde{\alpha}} \colon
        \Bx_{\langle n \rangle} \to \Bx_{\langle m \rangle}.$$
        % \item Let $C \in \Bx_{\langle m \rangle}$ and $C' \in \Bx_{\langle n
        % \rangle}$ be objects, $f \colon \langle m \rangle \to \langle n \rangle$
        % be a morphism in $\Brx$ and let $\Map_{\Bx}^f(C, C')$ be the union of
        % connected components of $\Map_{\Bx}(C, C')$ which lie over $f$. Choose
        % $p$-cocartesian morphisms $C' \to C'_i$ lying over the inert morphism
        % $\rho^i \colon \langle n \rangle \to \langle 1 \rangle$ for $1 \leq i
        % \leq n$. Then the induced map
        % \[
        %     \Map_{\Bx}^f(C, C') \to \prod_{1 \leq i \leq n} \Map_{\Bx}^{\rho^i \circ f}(C, C_i')
        % \]
        % is a homotopy equivalence.


    \item Let $C \in \Bx_{\langle n \rangle}$ and $C' \in \Bx_{\langle m \rangle}$
    be objects, $\widetilde{\alpha}  : \langle n \rangle \to \langle m \rangle$
    be a morphism in $\Brx$ and let $\Map_{\Bx}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}(X, X')$ be the 
    morphisms which lie over $\widetilde{\alpha}$. 
    Choose $\pi$-cocartesian morphisms $C' \to C'_i$ lying over the inert
    morphism $\rho^i \colon \langle n \rangle \to \langle 1 \rangle$ for $1 \leq
    i \leq n$. Then the induced map
    \[
        \Map_{\Bx}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}(C, C') \to \prod_{1 \leq i \leq n} \Map_{\Bx}^{\rho^i \circ \widetilde{\alpha}}(C, C_i')
    \]
    is an homotopy equivalence.
    
    \item For every finite collection of objects $C_1, \dots, C_n \in
    \Bx_{\langle 1 \rangle}$, there exists an object $C \in \D_{\langle n
    \rangle}$ and a collection of $\pi$-cocartesian morphism $C \to C_i$
    covering $\rho^i \colon \langle n \rangle \to \langle 1 \rangle$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{defin}
We suspect that this definition of a braided $\infty$-operad is equivalent to colored braided operad in $\Top$.
 
\medskip

Another interesting next step would be to study algebras over colored braided
operads. Similarily as we can define algebras over a colored symmetric operad
$\O$ valued in a symmetric monoidal category, we expect that we can define
algebras over a colored braided operad $\B$ valued in a braided monoidal
category.
Moreover, one could consider further examples of colored braided operads and their algebras.

Furthermore, we have seen the braid operad as an action operads in Section
\ref{sec:colbraidop}, which are the first step towards group operads following
\cite{yau}. An intriguing question that remains open is whether the framework
developed here can be generalized to other group operads. However, as our
framework is very much tailored to the structure of braidings, we do not see a
natural extension.
