\subsection{Braided monoidal structure}

In the following we will construct a braided monoidal structure obtained from a cofibration over $\Ex$. To do so we will need to choose paths in $\Ex$ on various occations. We will refrain from specifying the paths in detail but rather describe the paths enough to understand from which homotopy class we choose a path.
\begin{rem}
    Let us recall the definition of $\Ex$. For each $n \in \N$ we have an object $\langle n \rangle$. A mophism $\langle n \rangle \to \langle m \rangle$ in $\Ex$ consists of a morphism 
    $\alpha \colon \langle n \rangle \to \langle m \rangle$ in $\Fin$ and a collection of configurations $\{c_j \in \E_2(|\alpha^{-1}(j)|)\}_{1 \leq j \leq m}$ where each configuration is labeled by $\alpha^{-1}(j))$.
    So for example, let \begin{align*}
    \alpha \colon \langle 3 \rangle &\to \langle 2 \rangle \\
    1 &\mapsto 1 \\
    2 &\mapsto 2 \\
    3 &\mapsto 1 \\
    \end{align*}
    and
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tikzpicture}
                        % First element (left)
                        \draw[thick] (-3,0) circle (2);
                        \draw[thick] (-3.8, 1) circle (0.5);

                        \node at (-3.8, 1) {\small $1$};
                        \node at (-3, -0.5) {\small $3$};
                        \node at (0,-2) {,};
                        % Second element (right)
                        \draw[thick] (3,0) circle (2);
                        \draw[thick] (2,0.7) circle (0.5);
                        \node at (2,0.7) {\small $2$};
        \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{center}
    We denote the trivial configuration with label $x$ by $\id^x \in \E_2(1)$.
\end{rem}


\begin{defin}
    Let $\Cotimes$ be a category with a Grothendieck cofibration
    \[
        \Cotimes \to \Ex
    \]
    satisfying the (M2) condition:
    For $\C \coloneqq \Cotimes_{\langle 1 \rangle}$, we have $\Cotimes_{\langle n \rangle} \cong \C^n$. Then we call $\Cotimes$ with $\pi$ a $\E_2$-operad.
\end{defin}

Let $\pi \colon \Cotimes \to \Ex$ be a $\E_2$-operad. We denote $\C \coloneqq \Cotimes_{\langle 1 \rangle}$. In the following section we aim to define a braided monoidal structure on $\C$.

Note that for a diagram in $\Ex$
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\langle n \rangle \arrow[r, "c_1"] \arrow[rd, "c_2"] & \langle 1 \rangle \arrow[d, "\id"] \\
                                                     & \langle 1 \rangle                 
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with $c_1$ and $c_2$ being configurations in $\E_2(n)$, commutativity means choosing a path from $c_1$ to $c_2$.
Let $\beta$ denote the following morphism in $\Ex$
     \begin{align*}
         \beta \colon \langle 2 \rangle &\to \langle 2 \rangle \\
         1 &\mapsto 2 \\
         2 &\mapsto 1. \\
     \end{align*}
with the trivial collection $(\id^2, \id^1)$.
Further, let \[
\mu \colon \langle 2 \rangle \to \langle 1 \rangle
\] be the morphism with the following configuration which we refer to as the basepoint configuration:

Since $\pi$ satisfies (M1) and (M2), we have that for any $(A,B) \in \C^2 \cong \Cotimes_{\langle 2 \rangle}$ there is a cocartesian lift $(A,B) \to A \otimes B$ which defines a functor
\[
    \otimes \colon \C^2 \to \C.
\]

Then, we consider the following diagramm in $\Ex$
\[
    \begin{tikzcd}
        \langle 2 \rangle \arrow[r, "\beta"] \arrow[d, "\mu"] & \langle 2 \rangle \arrow[d, "\mu"] \\
        \langle 1 \rangle  \arrow[r, dashed]                  & \langle 1 \rangle.                 
    \end{tikzcd}
\]
Filling the diagram, i.e. giving commutativity means choosing a path from the basepoint configuration $\mu$ to the twisted basepoint configuration $\mu \circ \beta$. We choose this to be the half twist.

Note, that for a different choice of path instead of this half twist, we would obtain in the following a different braiding. So, this choice is essential for our construction.
Again using the (M1) property of $\pi$ we obtain a commuting diagram in $\Cotimes$

\[
\begin{tikzcd}
{(A,B)} \arrow[r, "\beta"] \arrow[d, "\otimes"] & {(B,A)} \arrow[d, "\otimes"] \\
A \otimes B \arrow[r, "b"]                  & B \otimes A                 
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The induced morphism $b$ is a unique isomorphism due to the fact that $\otimes$ and $\beta$ are cocartesian morphisms.
