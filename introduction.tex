\section{Introduction}



There are diﬀerent types of algebra: associative, commutative, Lie, etc. and corresponding to them an appropriate notion of a module.
It is often required to deal with these “up to homotopy”.

There was a needs to develop abstract frameworks in which to study algebras. One of which 
was introduced by May in the 1970s in \cite{may} to study iterated loop spaces - operads.
An operad is a mathematical structure that encodes operations with multiple
inputs and a single output, along with rules for composing these operations.
Operads have since been established as a fundamental framework across different fields 
They provide a way
to understand algebraic structures where operations can be composed in tree-like
patterns.
The power of operads lies in their ability to capture the essence of
"operations and their symmetries" in a purely combinatorial way, while
simultaneously encoding topological and algebraic information.

The classical theory of operads focuses on symmetric operads—structures where
the symmetric group actions capture the permutability of inputs. However, a
growing body of work has sought to generalize operads by relaxing or modifying
the symmetry conditions. In particular, braided operads arise as a natural
intermediate between symmetric and nonsymmetric operads. Furthermore, braided operads have 
found important applications; for example, Fiedorowicz established recognition principles in \cite{EO}
that depend on the braided structure of a braided
operad.
% These structures
% accommodate a notion of braiding—modeled by the braid group rather than the

% operations. Such generalizations are especially relevant in contexts where
% inputs to operations are not fully interchangeable, such as in quantum algebra
% or braided monoidal categories.
This thesis investigates the theory of braided operads and extends it to a new
notion: colored braided operads.
The goal is to provide both conceptual clarity
and a structural foundation for these objects, with an emphasis on categorical
formulations. 
In particular, we follow a guiding principle: that operads can be described using functors
that satisfy certain properties. This perspective shifts the focus from
traditional presentations in terms of sets, groups, and actions, toward a more
abstract, categorical formulation, ultimately leading to a more elegant and
flexible framework.

\medskip
Section 2 begins with a general overview of operads. We get introduced to the
usual notion of operads such as algebras and modules over an operad. Further, we see some common examples.
After we established a good foundation, we
develop the theory from
a categorical standpoint following Jacob Lurie in Higher Algebra \cite{ha}. We show how operads can be understood as functors
satisfying specific properties. This
Section serves both as a review and as a foundation for the later parts.


\medskip
Section 3 introduces braided operads, starting with the motivation behind
replacing the symmetric group with the braid group. We define braided operads
formally and provide illustrative examples that highlight their distinct
features.

\medskip
Section 4 proposes our own definition of colored braided operads, inspired by the
need to manage multiple colors of inputs and outputs while
retaining the braided structure. However, the aim in this Section
is to establish a further definition based on functorial properties and categorical
data — mirroring the approach taken in Section 2. A key factor is to find
the target category for the later categorical definition for which the paper on braid groups by Fox and Neuwirth \cite{fox}
will be useful.
We then move on to 
our first definition of a colored braided operad before we proceed to define a forgetful functor that is similar to the forgetful functor from Section 2. 
Before investigating its properties, we see how it is related to braided monoidal structures.
Finally, we verify the properties of the forgetful functor and see that this in fact gives an alternative
definition for colored braided operads.

\medskip
This opens the door for further applications and generalizations to the $\infty$-categorical context.
By the end of the thesis, we aim to have established a clear and coherent
framework for thinking about braided and colored operads in categorical terms.
The hope is that this perspective not only sheds light on the structures
themselves but also provides
tools and language that will be useful in future
